# From book to short video

## 0 — Choose and verify

Confirm the exact edition and author. Make a compact source ledger: source title, URL/file, publication date, claim supported, and confidence. For reviews and reader highlights, use them as signals about reader interest—not as copy to lift.

## 1 — Draft for a viewer, not a catalog

Start with one specific tension, question, or moment a viewer recognizes. Build one idea per beat: hook → concrete book detail → why it matters to the viewer → honest recommendation. Keep spoilers and factual claims intentional. Do not promise that the book will change someone's life or guarantee a result.

Split speech into short, natural subtitle-sized segments. Save exact approved speech in `script.md`; the storyboard must point back to those segment IDs verbatim.

## 2 — Review gate

Show the full script and source notes before visual production. Wait for an explicit approval. If the creator revises a line, update its sources and scene mapping before continuing.

## 3 — Storyboard and media

For every spoken segment, write what viewers should see and why it belongs at that moment. Prefer a legible primary visual over several tiny inserts. Evidence (edition, quote, author fact) should be shown large enough to read on a phone. Track original, generated, licensed, and user-provided media distinctly.

## 4 — Voice and subtitles

Use the creator's voice recording or an explicitly approved voice source. Do not synthesize a real person's voice without permission. Align captions to the approved narration, not to an automatic transcript. Optional English translation is a second line only when the composition has room.

## 5 — Preview, render, publish

Preview first at 9:16 on a phone-sized frame. Inspect the opening 3 seconds and every evidence-bearing frame. Validate that each visual matches its line; nothing is cut off, unreadable, duplicated, or hidden by platform UI. Check voice/edit continuity and music rights. Export only after approval, then re-open the actual output and inspect it again.

## Quality gate

An episode is not release-ready until all are true:

- Title, author, and edition are verified.
- Every factual sales claim has a source, and every direct quotation has edition/location context.
- Creator approved the exact spoken script.
- Every spoken segment maps to one storyboard entry with a concrete relevant visual.
- All media have provenance/rights notes.
- Chinese captions match narration; time ranges are valid and non-overlapping.
- Mobile preview and final render have been visually checked.
